# Plan v2: an original Klingon vocalist sings Hurt

**Current plan, 2026-09-20. Planning only; production has not started.** This supersedes [v1](PLAN.md)'s Cash-voice and original-backing requirements. The owner retains approval over production, spending, artistic choices, and release. Source references resolve to the dated [source register](SOURCES.md).

**Recommendation:** perform the vocal with the owner's or a consenting collaborator's own voice, develop an original Klingon character through pronunciation and delivery, and build fresh accompaniment with played instruments or MIDI. Keep the recognizable melody and a Cash-cover-inspired musical structure and mood. Use light audio processing only if it improves the result.

**Feasibility:** substantially more practical than v1. Neither a Cash model nor studio stems are needed. The main uncertainties are singable Klingon, the performer's skills, song recognition after adaptation, and the still-undefined parody commentary. No empirical success percentage is justified before a pilot.

**Budget direction:** aim for **$0-100 incremental cash** with existing equipment and DIY labor; optional language help is a separate choice. Recommend a first pilot using owned tools, **no purchases and at most six working hours**. These are proposed limits, not spending or production authorization.

Verify structure and melody through lawful listening or an appropriately obtained score once production is approved. Transpose the whole arrangement into a comfortable range while retaining melodic intervals. Do not force the singer into Cash's register.

There is no single real-world "Klingon voice" to reproduce. Choose an original character's register, resonance, emotional stance, and articulation. A restrained or vulnerable Klingon is compatible with this brief; constant shouting or heavy distortion is not required. ## 2. Recommended DIY workflow

1. **Choose the artistic branch.** State whether the piece comments on Hurt/Cash's interpretation, faithfully adapts the song into Klingon, or uses it for broader satire. The branches below leave the message to the owner. Select a provisional rights basis and intended audience before using the composition in production.
2. **Check owned resources.** Identify the singer, recording device, headphones, quiet space, instrument or MIDI setup, and existing editor. Make the cheapest workable chain the default. A basic recording setup is enough to test the concept; expensive hardware cannot fix an unclear melody or language.
3. **Run the short pilot described in section 5.** First test character/diction on original material. After the composition-use decision, test a newly performed target excerpt for recognition. Neither test is authorized by this document.
4. **Map the music.** Build a compact section map with phrase entrances, principal melody notes, harmony, rests, and a dynamic arc. Retain recognizable phrase shapes, especially at salient entries. Adjust key and tempo for the singer, then freeze the pilot-approved map. Do not demand sample-accurate timing against Cash's master.
5. **Adapt the words for singing.** Keep a literal meaning draft separate from the sung adaptation. For parody, the owner must first approve the intended commentary; fidelity is then to that approved artistic brief, not necessarily every original proposition. Check a back-translation and fit syllables to the melody before polishing rhyme.
6. **Build fresh backing.** Overdub an instrument, or enter MIDI notes and render an instrument sound. A simple synthesizer can establish timing and recognition before an acoustic-style sound is chosen. Use owned or appropriately licensed instruments/samples; no copying audio from the Cash recording or an unofficial karaoke track. MIDI rendering is a controllable way to regenerate accompaniment without a text-to-music service.
7. **Record and edit the character vocal.** Record short phrases, select good takes, and align them to the approved phrase map. Keep an untreated vocal. Start with level, equalization, compression, and modest ambience; compare any pitch/formant/texture processing at matched loudness. Keep the unprocessed take if processing damages consonants or emotion.
8. **Mix and review.** Let the words remain audible over the accompaniment. Check recognition, language, artifacts, and the intended mood. Make at most two focused revision passes before deciding whether more time is worthwhile.
9. **Deliver privately, then decide release separately.** Export a lossless master and listening copy with contributor credits and accurate performance disclosure. Public release requires owner approval of the actual work, its rights position, and destination; a successful technical pilot is not legal clearance.

Audacity is a free, open-source option for recording, overdubbing, editing, and mixing audio. REAPER is an optional integrated audio/MIDI route with tempo, pitch, virtual-instrument, and mixing support. Use an already owned DAW if it does the job. REAPER's basic bundled synthesizer is sufficient for a sketch; a convincing sampled piano or guitar may require an instrument library whose license must be checked. Nothing has been installed. [S23](SOURCES.md#s23), [S24](SOURCES.md#s24)

## 3. Klingon and performance checks

Preserve standard romanized spelling and case. `q` and `Q` are distinct; `'` is a glottal stop; `ng` and `tlh` must not be read as English letter sequences. Include uvular, fricative, lateral, and glottal articulation in the future pilot, especially `q`, `Q`, `H`, `gh`, `tlh`, and `'`. A low or rough voice is not a substitute for these distinctions. [S06](SOURCES.md#s06), [S07](SOURCES.md#s07)

For each sung phrase, mark syllables, stress, vowel sustain, consonant preparation/release, and breaths. Preserve meaningful consonants when stretching a vowel or joining notes. Rephrase an awkward sung line instead of crushing extra syllables into the beat. A literal translation alone does not solve melodic fit; that is the separate work of singable adaptation. [S09](SOURCES.md#s09)

DIY drafting is appropriate, but an automatic translation or an English speech recognizer cannot certify Klingon accuracy. Recommend one fluent Klingon listener for a short blind transcription/comprehension check, ideally a volunteer collaborator; a paid specialist or second reviewer is optional. KLI written credentials help identify language experience but do not certify singing proficiency. [S08](SOURCES.md#s08)

Before giving a reviewer the intended text, ask what they hear and what it means. Compare spoken, dry sung, and mixed versions. Check the back-translation against the approved semantic/parody brief. If no fluent reviewer is available, keep the language status **unverified** and let the owner decide whether a rough fan experiment is sufficient. Do not claim linguistically verified Klingon on self-review alone.

## 5. Future short pilot and acceptance tests

**Pilot A, original material:** after separate approval, use 10-20 seconds of newly written or user-supplied authorized words and music. Record a spoken diagnostic plus a sung phrase with representative Klingon sounds, a sustained vowel, and a quiet/strong contrast. Compare the dry voice with one light processing treatment; optionally compare one eligible synthetic route. This tests diction and character without using Hurt.

**Pilot B, target recognition:** only after the owner selects the artistic branch and composition-use basis, newly perform approximately 20-40 seconds spanning a recognizable melodic phrase and a useful dynamic contrast. This uses the composition but none of Cash's recorded audio. Test the accompaniment/guide first, then the Klingon vocal. The duration is a production test size, **not a legal safe harbor**. Choose the amount according to the artistic purpose and rights analysis.

Suggested combined cap: six working hours and $0 new purchases, with one revision within the cap. If unavailable equipment, language knowledge, or performance skill prevents a meaningful test, return a specific request for help or a revised cap. Do not silently lower the quality target.

| Gate | Proposed practical pass criterion | What failure means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Song recognition | At least 4 of 5 listeners already familiar with Hurt name the song from an untitled target clip without leading hints | Restore salient melody/phrase/harmonic cues before adding production polish; these counts are a small usability check, not a population estimate |
| Cash-cover-inspired treatment | Owner plus two listeners judge the pacing, space, structure, and emotional build consistent with the brief; all rate fit at least 3/5 | Revise arrangement/delivery; do not solve it by imitating Cash's identity |
| Sung language | Recommended verified-language gate: a fluent reviewer identifies at least 90% of words in dry and mixed listening, with no unresolved meaning-changing sound errors | Re-record or re-adapt; if no reviewer is available, mark language unverified rather than claiming a pass |
| Character and expression | Owner rates character fit at least 4/5 and intelligibility/naturalness at least 3/5; no intended resemblance to a named real performer | Reduce processing or choose a different original delivery |
| Melody and rhythm | No unintended note changes in recognizable phrases; words fit without rushed endings or lost consonants; no cumulative drift against the new backing | Fix phrase allocation or guide performance; transposition and deliberate new timing are allowed |
| Sound and files | No clipped samples, complete tails, no obvious warble/clicks, audible words on headphones/speakers/mono | Repair the identified defect, then recheck the affected passage |
| Production/release basis | Owner approves the artistic branch, documented permission or fair-use position as appropriate, contributor/tool conditions, and intended use | Resolve the specific issue before the dependent work; no assumption that all branches require a license |

For the full track, repeat language and audio checks throughout and use more than one excerpt for recognition. No Cash-similarity score is needed. A newly recorded backing eliminates the stem-separation bleed problem rather than funding attempts to repair it.

## 6. Fair use, parody, and the remaining rights questions

The legal term is **fair use**. Noncommercial purpose can help the analysis, but neither noncommercial use nor translating into Klingon automatically establishes it. This plan identifies alternative routes and questions, not a conclusion that the project is lawful or unlawful. U.S. law is the working framework; a different release jurisdiction may require a different analysis. [S20](SOURCES.md#s20)

In *Campbell*, the Supreme Court described parody's justification as using an earlier work to comment, at least partly, on that work. Merely calling the project parody or making the singer fictional does not establish the necessary relationship. Broader satire can also be fair use, but its borrowing needs its own justification. Recognition can serve parody; it is not permission to copy everything without considering purpose and extent. [S21](SOURCES.md#s21)

For this project, if the target is Cash's interpretation, explain why borrowing Reznor's composition is needed for that commentary. The plan leaves that artistic justification open.

| Artistic branch, still provisional | Decision needed | Available production basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Parody of the song or its interpretation** | Identify the actual target/commentary and how the finished work communicates it; do not invent that message in this plan | Owner may elect a documented fair-use position after assessing the actual work, borrowing, and intended use; express permission is an alternative |
| **Faithful Klingon translation/cover** | Confirm that the point is a new-language rendition rather than commentary on the original | Express adaptation permission is the clearer route; any asserted fair use needs independent support, not the parody label |
| **Broader satire or playful transformation** | Explain what it comments on and why borrowing Hurt is relevant | Case-specific fair-use analysis or permission; avoid treating satire as automatically protected or automatically excluded |

Apply all four statutory factors together, to the proposed finished use:

| Factor | Project-specific question |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose and character | What commentary or distinct purpose is perceptible, and how does the noncommercial presentation support it? |
| Nature of the original | This is a published creative song, not factual reference material; how does that bear on the proposed use? |
| Amount and substantiality | Which melodic/lyrical/arrangement elements and how much duration are necessary? A full-song adaptation needs an explanation beyond recognition. |
| Market effect | Could this substitute for the original or relevant licensed adaptations? Assess actual distribution and likely effects; criticism-induced disfavor differs from substitution. |

The four-factor framework comes from section 107 and Copyright Office guidance. *Campbell* distinguishes critical effects from market substitution. *Warhol* further cautions that new expression or meaning alone does not decide the first factor; evaluate the specific use. None supplies an automatic result here. [S04](SOURCES.md#s04), [S20](SOURCES.md#s20), [S21](SOURCES.md#s21), [S22](SOURCES.md#s22)

**What v2 removes:** if every sound is newly recorded/rendered and no real person's identity is imitated, this route does not depend on licensing Cash's master or voice identity. **What remains:** composition/adaptation questions, any protectable arrangement material actually borrowed, consent of new performers, and instrument/model/service conditions. Sound-recording and composition rights are distinct; an ordinary cover/mechanical license does not provide a general translation permission. [S02](SOURCES.md#s02), [S03](SOURCES.md#s03), [S04](SOURCES.md#s04)

Before target production, keep a short private note of the chosen branch, actual intended commentary, borrowed elements, audience, distribution, four-factor reasoning or permission scope, and open questions. If relying on fair use, targeted advice from a qualified lawyer is an option before public release, not a mandatory professional-budget line or a requirement to obtain a license regardless of the facts. Platform contracts and takedown processes remain separate practical considerations; owner approval does not settle legality.

Credit Reznor's songwriting and describe Cash's cover as the musical reference. Suggested factual disclosure: **"Noncommercial fan reinterpretation with an original fictional Klingon vocalist and newly performed backing; not a Johnny Cash performance."** Add synthetic-vocal disclosure if applicable. Do not claim official endorsement, rights-holder authorization, or legal clearance that has not been obtained. Attribution/disclosure alone does not establish fair use.

## 8. Inputs, outputs, and delivery

Inputs are the approved artistic brief, lawfully accessed musical reference/score, owner-approved composition-use basis, privately held working adaptation, new vocal recordings, and newly played or MIDI-rendered instrument parts. Record contributor consent and instrument/service licenses proportionately. The public repository receives none of these production assets.

Use one session sample rate. Prefer **48 kHz/24-bit WAV** for new recording when supported; preserve a device's genuine source quality rather than suggesting upsampling adds detail. Keep a dry mono vocal, separate backing tracks, full tails, and editable session. Export a **stereo lossless WAV master** at the session rate and a **320 kb/s MP3 listening copy** encoded from it. A vendor's compressed output does not become lossless source audio merely by exporting WAV.

Use a proposed **-1 dBTP maximum true peak** where the tools provide true-peak metering, and check the encoded listening copy for overs or artifacts. Prioritize dynamics and intelligibility over maximum loudness. Record basic mix/version notes and credit/disclosure text. Owner acceptance of a private delivery is separate from approval to upload or distribute it.
